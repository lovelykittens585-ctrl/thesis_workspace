THIS IS NOT THE ABSTRACT

I'm online, yes, but not in the sense that I’m interacting with people on social media or watching tons of YouTube video essays or surfing Reddit and posting shit on my blog or this app.
If you know me,, you probably know that I’m not here much and that's for good reason! The typically reasons: keeping my emotional/mental in check, curbing a social media addiction and parasocial tendencies, etc etc the usual
There’s one particular reason I want to expand on because it has less to do with the social/emotional/mental and more about the creative and political aspects of being “chronically online”.

Not being online gives me time to breathe.

Breathing has less to do with my own health and more with how I am interacting with people, processing information and interacting with the media (generally). I’ve come to realize a few things as I’ve grown up and that's the general reactionary and instantly gratifying nature of the digital space. A space like this rewards quick remarks, responses and interactions. In this quickness we forget how to actually engage with the media we’re always consuming and people around us.
And this isn’t to say “go touch grass” or “get off your damn phone”, it’s more of an examination call to try and more meaningfully engage with the digital space rather than treat it like a McDonalds.
(Yeah it’s enjoyable and quick but what do u really get out of it other than the shits and guilt. Chill out a second girl, no one’s gonna take that Big Mac away from u)
I realize that we have an issue with two things: Breathing and making EVERYTHING a moment. (Am I making this a moment? No. This is an addendum)

We don’t stop to think and we want to intellectualize the hell out of everything. On the second part: Intellect does not equal understanding. You could be knowledgeable about a shit ton of things and still not really get/understand what you’re talking about. Additionally, and on the first part: the lack of breathing results in us thinking cuz we “got our degrees’ and think we know every-damn-thing” that we understand the world around us and people who occupy the spaces we live in.
We don’t. And we probs won’t for a hot minute. This is ok.

It becomes less ok, when we start to use our so-called “intellect” to step over each other in spaces we’re supposed to share.
Breathing isn’t just stopping to think, it considers what you’re giving and receiving from the space you’re in. It’s about labor(emotionally; sometimes physically), but also: charity. When no one breathes we don’t consider either and this sets a dangerous precedent for the rest of the landscape we’re supposedly living in.
(And be honest with yourself, how much time do you spend online and how much of it do you spend not being online but thinking about being online?)
Everyone’s fucking suffocating dude!!! It’s bad, everything’s gotta have a reaction . Everything’s gotta be satisfying or gratifying. We have to post post post post and posture posture posture and hopefully, maybe, we can make something of ourselves instead of having to really do anything that means something even if that so-called meaning is arbitrary!!!!

And the slim few that ARE breathing probably aren’t here,, neither in the digital or physical sense. (Maybe somewhere in-between)


I'm writing my thesis about the internet.

I spent most of my life on the internet being a ghost.

I’ve made two online friends since I’ve been online.

I engage with content like everyone else but I don’t comment, I don’t say anything because the internet is seemingly forever and I’m deathly afraid of being aired out. But I’m writing this thesis because I care about the internet, despite my own personal relationship with it. I believe in it sorta,, the potential it has and how people can come together despite being millions of miles apart from each other.

It's less about coming together and more about finding your own, personal, intimate relationship with this space.

I think we get so up in arms about how shitty this space can be and forget to ask ourselves why we even fight so hard to occupy space here in the first place. I'm not saying I’d die for this shit, but I wouldn’t say it’s a lost cause either.

So, I'm asking you to join me in this exploration in trying to understand where we're at and where we're going.
